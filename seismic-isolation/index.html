Fredericton sits just 20 meters above the St. John River, on ground that shakes differently than the bedrock outcrops up in the UNB hill. The 2015 Miramichi aftershock sequence reminded a lot of people here that felt reports travel fast across soft sediments. A building anchored straight into the soil absorbs every jolt. With base isolation seismic design we decouple the structure from the ground, letting the isolators take the displacement while the building stays almost still. It is not a luxury for this city — it is a rational answer to deep soft soils that amplify long-period motion, something our team addresses using MASW profiles to define the shear wave contrast down to refusal before any isolator type is selected.

Isolation does not reduce the seismic hazard — it redefines the demand path so the structure absorbs less energy per cycle.

Technical details of the service in Fredericton

In Fredericton we regularly see designs where the isolation plane gets placed just above the foundation mat or below the first suspended slab, depending on the water table. The St. John River floodplain is unforgiving; any isolator housing must stay dry. We favor lead-rubber bearings for their compact re-centering capability, but high-damping rubber works well when vertical loads are moderate. The seismic microzonation maps we produce before schematic design show where basin-edge effects amplify the fundamental period, and that drives the displacement demand calculation. Our approach ties directly to NBCC 2020 spectral values, adjusting site coefficients with measured Vs30, not generic table values. We also check for uplift potential in corner columns — a common oversight in early Fredericton isolation schemes — and specify moat covers that handle the 1000-year return period displacement without binding.

Typical technical challenges in Fredericton

NBCC 2020 Part 4 requires that base-isolated structures meet the same collapse prevention goal as fixed-base buildings, but through a fundamentally different mechanism. In Fredericton the risk is not just the shaking — it is the soft clay beneath the north side of downtown. If the isolation period creeps too close to the site period during moderate shaking, amplification can cause isolator excursions beyond the design envelope. We run nonlinear time-history analysis with scaled ground motions that match the uniform hazard spectrum for the city. The analysis must prove that the moat wall is not struck under MCE, and that utilities crossing the isolation plane have flexible loops rated for the full displacement. Overlooking the utility detailing has caused more post-earthquake downtime in Canada than isolator failure itself.

Does NBCC mandate base isolation for any building type?

NBCC 2020 does not mandate base isolation for any occupancy class, but it recognizes isolation as a proven method to achieve post-disaster performance for essential facilities. For high-importance buildings in Fredericton, the cost-benefit analysis often favors isolation over a fixed-base ductile design.

How do you handle the soft soil conditions along the St. John River?

We run a site-specific response analysis using measured shear wave profiles, not just the default NBCC site factors. If the soil column amplifies long-period motion, we adjust the isolator effective period to stay clear of resonance and specify a larger moat gap to handle the extra displacement.

What maintenance do base isolators require after installation?

Isolators need a visual inspection every five years and a detailed condition assessment after any significant seismic event. The moat must remain clear, and elastomeric bearings should be checked for creep and cracking. A maintenance manual specific to the building is part of the final documentation package.
