A recent foundation investigation near the floodplain off Lincoln Road ran into unexpected silt lenses at just 2.1 meters. The contractor had assumed uniform sandy gravel based on a 2018 borehole two lots over. That assumption almost cost the project its schedule. In Fredericton, where the Saint John River has reworked glacial deposits for millennia, soil gradation can flip within a single block. Our lab runs the full mechanical sieve stack plus hydrometer on every sample that goes below the No. 200 sieve. No shortcuts. When a test pit uncovers layered material or a drill rig brings up gray silty sand, we need to know exactly what fraction passes 75 microns. That number dictates drainage design, frost susceptibility under NBCC Division B, and whether the material qualifies as backfill. We also cross-check gradation against Atterberg limits when the fines content exceeds 12%, because a non-plastic silt and a fat clay behave nothing alike under load.

A complete hydrometer curve is not optional when 12% or more of your sample passes the No. 200 sieve.

Technical details of the service in Fredericton

The contrast between Marysville's coarse terrace gravels and the downtown river silts is stark. In Marysville, we often see D10 values above 0.4 mm and uniformity coefficients below 3, which means excellent drainage but poor compaction control if the contractor overworks the lift. Downtown, near the legislative building, the soil profile tells a different story. There, the 200-sieve wash routinely retains 40 to 60 percent fines, and the hydrometer curve flattens out around 2 microns, signaling active clay. This split-city geology means we never apply a single gradation envelope to two projects. The lab adjusts the sieve stack depending on whether we expect gravel-dominated or sand-dominated material, and we run the hydrometer with sodium hexametaphosphate dispersion per ASTM D422. For road base design on the Ring Road extension, pairing our gradation data with Proctor compaction curves gave the contractor a defensible moisture-density specification that held up through a wet October.

Typical technical challenges in Fredericton

NBCC 2015 Part 4 requires soil classification per the Unified Soil Classification System before assigning bearing resistance or seismic site class. In Fredericton, ignoring the hydrometer on a silty sample can misclassify an SM as an SP, which changes the site coefficient for seismic design under the National Building Code. That error compounds fast. A building designed for Site Class C that actually sits on Site Class D material will see base shear underestimated, and the structural engineer will never know until it is too late. Frost heave is the other silent risk. Silts with 15 to 30 percent passing 75 microns are prime candidates for ice lens formation during Fredericton winters, where the frost depth routinely reaches 1.8 meters. A gradation curve that misses the silt shoulder is a liability. We keep the hydrometer running year-round because frozen soil does not forgive a skipped test.

Our services

The lab runs three tiers of gradation testing depending on project scope. Each tier includes the full sieve stack. The hydrometer is added whenever visual inspection or wash results indicate more than trace fines.

Standard Mechanical Sieve Analysis

Full nest of sieves from 75 mm down to No. 200, dry or washed method per ASTM D6913. Includes gradation curve, Cu, Cc, and USCS classification with lab report stamped by the technician.

Hydrometer Analysis (Fine Fraction)

ASTM D422 hydrometer test on the minus No. 200 fraction using 152H hydrometer. Temperature-corrected readings at 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 15, 30, 60, 250, and 1440 minutes. Combined curve delivered within 72 hours of sample receipt.

Combined Sieve and Hydrometer Package

Full particle-size distribution from gravel to clay. Recommended for all foundation investigations in Fredericton where the soil profile includes glaciomarine or alluvial deposits. Report includes D10 through D60, percent gravel-sand-silt-clay, and Atterberg correlation note.

How long does the hydrometer test take?

The hydrometer sedimentation test requires a minimum of 24 hours of readings, but we continue to 72 hours for samples with significant clay content to capture the full fine tail. Rush turnaround in 48 hours is available with a surcharge.

Do I need the hydrometer if the sample looks sandy?

Visual classification is unreliable. We have seen samples that appeared to be clean sand produce 18% fines after washing. If the project involves foundation design, retaining structures, or frost protection, the hydrometer is strongly recommended per NBCC soil classification requirements.

What sample size do you need for a full sieve plus hydrometer test?

For gravelly soils we need at least 5 kg of material. For sands and silty sands, 1 kg is sufficient. The hydrometer portion requires approximately 100 g of minus No. 200 material, so the original sample must contain enough fines after sieving.

How do you handle organic soils from the Saint John River floodplain?

Organic soils require pretreatment. We oven-dry at 60°C to avoid burning organic matter, then disaggregate gently. If the sample contains visible peat or fibrous material, we note it on the report and may recommend loss-on-ignition testing for accurate organic content quantification.
