Fredericton's subsurface conditions often require more than standard SPT data. The city sits on a complex mix of floodplain alluvium from the Saint John River, glacial till, and occasional soft clay lenses that can compromise shallow foundations. For a continuous soil profile, we rely on the Cone Penetration Test under ASTM D5778. This electronic method measures tip resistance, sleeve friction, and dynamic pore pressure in a single push — no borehole disturbance, no sample recovery issues. In areas like the old river terrace near downtown, we have seen how a liquefaction assessment becomes critical when the CPT detects loose saturated silts at depth. The test also feeds directly into pile design parameters for deep foundations in zones where the till is too dense for standard drilling.

A single CPT sounding can replace half a dozen disturbed SPT samples, giving you a high-resolution log without gaps.

Technical details of the service in Fredericton

Fredericton's seasonal extremes — from frozen ground in February to saturated soils during spring freshet — demand a solid characterization method. The CPT rig we operate cuts through frost-susceptible silts and weathered bedrock interfaces without the delays of auger refusal. On a recent assignment near the University of New Brunswick campus, the pore pressure dissipation tests identified a thin drainage layer within a clay unit, something a split-spoon sample would have missed entirely. This level of detail matters when you are designing retaining walls along the riverbank, where hydrostatic pressure can shift after heavy rain. Typical technical challenges in Fredericton

A common oversight we see in Fredericton's outlying developments is assuming uniform soil behavior across a site. The Saint John River has shifted course over centuries, leaving buried paleochannels filled with soft organic silt. A CPT profile that suddenly drops in tip resistance is your early warning. Skip this test and you risk differential settlement that no amount of structural reinforcement can fix cheaply. On one industrial lot in the Barker's Point area, our CPT rig revealed an undetected 4-meter band of sensitive clay; the design team switched from spread footings to a raft foundation, avoiding what could have been a costly post-construction repair. Cone refusal on shallow bedrock also helps define excavation limits precisely, reducing overbreak and contractor claims.

Common questions

How much does a CPT test cost in Fredericton?

For a standard CPTu sounding in the Fredericton area, budget approximately CA$230 to CA$320 per meter mobilized. The final figure depends on total meterage, site access (especially on soft riverbank terrain), and whether you add seismic or dissipation modules. We provide a firm quote after reviewing your site plan.

Can the CPT rig penetrate the glacial till common around Fredericton?

It can handle stiff till up to a point. Once the cone tip hits a dense boulder pavement or shallow bedrock, it will reach refusal — typically between 15 and 25 meters depth in the Fredericton uplands. At that point we record the refusal depth and recommend switching to a triaxial test on rock core if needed.

What advantage does CPT have over SPT for riverbank projects?

CPT gives you a continuous pressure and friction record. On the Saint John River banks, thin sand seams within the clay can go undetected by a 1.5-meter SPT spoon. Missing those seams means underestimating drainage and slope instability risks, a mistake we help our clients avoid with the cone's high-resolution data.

How fast can you mobilize a CPT rig in the Fredericton region?

We keep our CPT equipment in the Maritimes and can typically be on-site within three to five business days for urban Fredericton locations. Remote sites along the river or in harder-to-reach areas may require a short lead time for access planning, but we move fast once the scope is confirmed.

Do I still need a borehole if I run CPT soundings?

Often, yes — but fewer of them. A common strategy around Fredericton is to drill one borehole for sample recovery and index testing, then complement it with three or four CPT soundings to fill in the gaps between boreholes. This gives you both lab data and a nearly continuous subsurface model.
